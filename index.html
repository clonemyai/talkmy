You install it. You give one sentence.

Talkmy’s real first user is the parent, but its customer is you. You put it on her phone, and your whole tutorial is one sentence: “You just use it like we chat, like a person, and you can send photos. I installed it for you. It looks like this.” The junk mail, the letters, the what-does-this-charge-mean: the app takes those now. What still reaches you is what deserves you: the judgement calls.

A named person, kept on her phone

If she wants, Talkmy remembers one family member for assistance, picked by her from her own contacts and kept only in the capsule on her phone. When something needs a human check, Talkmy can name that person and suggest she call them. Talkmy never contacts them on its own. You stay load-bearing. The friction goes.
