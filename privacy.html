Privacy Policy

Talkmy™, the AI for everyone. In effect from November 18, 2026.

The short version: Talkmy has no account and no sign-in, no advertising, and no tracking. What Talkmy remembers about you stays in a small file on your own phone, and a few facts from that file travel with your questions so the answers can fit you. When you hold the Talk button and speak, a short clip of your voice, like a voice message, is sent to Google's AI service, which listens to it and makes the answer. A photo, if you are asking about one, is sent with it. We keep no copy, and nothing is tied to your name.

Listening

Talkmy only hears you while you are holding the Talk button. While you hold it, Talkmy records a short clip of your voice, like a voice message. The microphone is never on at any other time, and Talkmy never listens in the background.

How answers are made

When you let go of the Talk button, your voice clip is sent to a small Talkmy cloud function, run for Talkmy on Google's Firebase. The function passes the clip to Google's Gemini AI, which listens to it, writes down what you said, and makes the answer. What you said and the answer appear on your screen. The last few exchanges of your conversation are sent along as context, so a follow-up question still makes sense. Talkmy stores no copy of your conversation on any server. The conversation itself is kept on your own phone, inside the app, so it is still there when you come back to it, and the photos in it are kept there too, at the smaller size the app works with, in the app's own storage and never in your Photos library. That history never leaves your phone, and you can erase it whenever you like by asking Talkmy to clear the chat: it asks you once first, and clearing erases the conversation only, never the things it remembers about you.

If you are asking about a photo, the photo is sent with your clip. If you type instead of speaking, your typed words are sent as text to the same Gemini AI, and the answer comes back as words on your screen. You can hear it by tapping the round button beside it.

When you spoke your question, the answer is read aloud. Usually Google's speaking service, Google Cloud Text-to-Speech, makes the voice. It receives only the answer's own words, never your voice. If that service cannot be reached, your phone's own built-in voice reads the answer instead.

Your clip is used only to answer that one question. Under the terms of the paid Google services Talkmy uses, what you send is not used to train Google's models. Talkmy's own cloud functions keep nothing: there is no database and no stored copy of your clips, your questions, your photos, or the answers. Their logs record only numbers, like how long a clip was, never what was said. The page the code opens counts its arrivals the same way, by the kind of phone and by whether a code brought the visit; the codes are the same for everyone and carry nothing about any person.

No account, and the plumbing behind it

Talkmy never asks for a name, an email, or a password, and there is no account to sign in to. Behind the scenes, so Google's services can be reached safely, the app signs itself in anonymously and automatically. That anonymous identity is a random code made by Firebase. It is not your name, and it is not linked to anything about you. The app also proves it is the real Talkmy app through Apple's app attestation, a check that keeps other programs from using Talkmy's services.

Talkmy also keeps a short random number for your install, made up on your phone and kept in your capsule. It stays on your phone. It leaves only inside a bug report you choose to send, described below, so the maker can tell one phone's reports from another's.

Your voice

Talkmy does not use Apple's speech recognition at all, and your phone's Dictation setting makes no difference to Talkmy. Your voice reaches the answer only as the short clip described above. That clip is used once, to answer the question you just asked. It is deleted from your phone when the hold finishes, and no copy of it is kept.

When there is no internet

Talkmy needs the internet to answer. If there is no connection, Talkmy cannot answer, and it will tell you so in one calm line, in your language. It does not fall back to answering on the phone itself. There is no offline mode. When the internet comes back, Talkmy works again, just as before.

Your capsule

Talkmy remembers a few things so it can be helpful: your language, the place you call home, how you like things (such as bigger words on the screen, bigger or smaller buttons, a different or slower voice, and short or longer answers), and any price you ask it to save for you. It learns them from what you say and what you choose: from the words Gemini writes down from your clips, from words you type, and from changes you ask for out loud, like "slow down" or "make the words bigger."

Those remembered things live in a small file, called a capsule, stored on your own phone. The file itself is never sent anywhere. A few of its facts, your language, your home place, and how you like your answers, are included with each question so the answer can fit you. If you delete Talkmy, the capsule is deleted with it.

Your photos

A photo you take or choose is held in your conversation for the question you ask with it, and sent with that question to be answered, as described above. A photo you take with Talkmy's own camera is not saved into your photo library, and Talkmy keeps no copy of any photo of its own.

Weather

Talkmy can answer questions about the weather where you live. It learns where home is only from what you tell it, in your own words, like "I live in Folsom." Talkmy never uses GPS, and it never asks your phone for your location. The place you named is looked up once, through a map lookup service run by Open-Meteo, to find the map coordinates for it, and those coordinates are kept in your capsule. When a forecast is needed, the coordinates are sent to a weather service, Open-Meteo, or in the United States the National Weather Service, and the forecast that comes back is included with your question so the answer can be in your language.

A family member for assistance

Talkmy may ask whether you would like to add a family member who can help you when needed. If you say yes, you pick that person yourself in your phone's own contact picker. Talkmy never looks through your address book. It sees only the one person you pick, and keeps their name, how they are related or known to you, their phone number, and their email in your capsule, on your phone, and nowhere else. When answers are made, only their name and that description are included as context, so Talkmy can suggest the right person when something needs a human check. Their phone number and email are never included. Talkmy does not contact them on its own.

Opening other apps

Sometimes Talkmy can offer to open another app for you: YouTube to watch a video, or a maps app for directions. It always shows a big card first that reads the search or the place back to you, and nothing opens until you tap the card or say yes. When YouTube opens, the search words you asked for go to YouTube. When a maps app opens, Apple Maps, or Google Maps if you have it and choose it, the place you named goes to that app. From that moment, those apps handle things under their own privacy policies.

Sharing Talkmy

The Talkmy name at the top left of the screen is also a sharing button, with a small bubble under it that says so in your language. Tapping it opens your phone's share sheet, carrying a short message about Talkmy in your language and a link to talkmy.ai. Tapping it first opens a share card: a big QR code, made on your phone, that another phone can scan to reach Talkmy, with the address written under it and a button that opens the share sheet. Nothing is sent until you choose a person or an app in that sheet, and what is sent is only that message and that link. It includes nothing about you and nothing from your conversations.

If something goes wrong

If you tell Talkmy something is wrong, it will ask you to hold Talk and say what happened, in your own words. Talkmy writes your words down and adds a few technical notes: the language you use, the short random number for your install, and recent notes about how the app has been working, such as how long a hold lasted, whether an answer arrived, a place you asked directions to, or the weather line used for an answer. They never include the words of your other questions or of the answers, only counts of how long those were. Before anything is sent, Talkmy tells you in the conversation that the report is ready. It then opens your own Mail app with the report addressed to the people who make Talkmy, at vivian@clonemy.ai. You tap Send yourself. Nothing is sent behind your back. If Mail is not set up on your phone, the report simply stays as words in your conversation, and nothing is sent at all.

No tracking

Talkmy has no advertising, no analytics, and no trackers. There are no advertising or analytics tools built into the app. The only report that ever leaves your phone is a bug report you choose to send yourself, as described above.

Everyone

Talkmy asks no one for an account, a name, or an age. The same rules on this page apply to everyone who uses it.

Changes

If this policy ever changes, the new words will appear on this page with a new date, before they take effect.
